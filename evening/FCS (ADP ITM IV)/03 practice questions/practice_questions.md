# Week 1: Finance for Computer Science – Student Questions

*Based on the Week 1 lecture slides*

---

1. **The Core Formula**  
   The presentation introduces a simple formula that "drives every business decision." What is this formula? Using the real-world example from the slides, calculate the profit for a mobile game company that earns $500,000 in revenue and spends $300,000 on expenses.

2. **The Five Principles**  
   The foundations of finance are built on five core principles. List all five principles and explain the main idea of "Principle 3: Cash Flow is King" in your own words.

3. **Time Value of Money Application**  
   A client offers to pay you $5,000 now or $5,500 in one year. According to the slide’s real-world example, under what investment return condition are both options equal? And what should you do if you can earn a 15% return, and why?

4. **Cash Flow vs. Profit**  
   The presentation states that "many profitable startups fail" and that you can be "profitable 'on paper' but still go bankrupt." Explain this critical difference between profit and cash flow, using the example of the startup with $1 million in sales that still failed.

5. **Principle 5: Diversification**  
   Explain the core idea behind the principle "Diversification Reduces Risk." Using Amazon as an example from the slides, describe how the company has diversified its business to protect itself.

6. **The Role of Finance in Decisions**  
   The slides describe a scenario where a company must choose between building "Option A: Dark Mode" and "Option B: AI Assistant." What is the financial process called that companies use to evaluate these choices, and what is the key question finance helps answer in this situation?

7. **Building a Budget**  
   You have a $50,000 budget to build a mobile app. Based on the budget breakdown in the presentation, if you spend $7,500 on design, $7,500 on marketing, and $7,500 on testing, how much money remains for the biggest category, which is development?

8. **Business Structures and Personal Risk**  
   Compare the three main legal forms of business organization (Sole Proprietorship, Partnership, Corporation) in terms of the owner's personal liability for business debts. Which structure protects your personal assets?

9. **Following the Startup Journey**  
   The presentation follows Alex and Sam. They start by forming a partnership for their app idea. Why did they later change their legal structure to a corporation when an investor offered them $500,000?

10. **Finance as a Bridge**  
    The presentation's "Big Picture" section describes finance as a bridge. In the real-world example of Instagram's founders, what were the specific actions they took (combining CS and finance skills) that led to the final business outcome?
